Looks like the bubble has finally popped

Oracle is down around 61% since I declared the excitement around its deal with OpenAI “peak bubble” last September, and Ben Thompson of Stratechery just wrote an “obituary”, declaring that “The demise of Jensen Huang was the end of a great many things, including Nvidia’s long-term prospects, the Al bubble, and the exceptional way in which the U.S. viewed itself in the world.”

Tech stocks continue to drop sharply today, following on the rout we saw last week. Even Axios seemed to acknowledge this morning (before the market opened) that the jig is up.

One big issue is that the “bank” that was financing it all, Nvidia, is unlikely to continue to doing so. In the words of a Wall Street Journal article yesterday, “Nvidia’s Days as an Open Checkbook for the AI Economy Are Numbered: Interim CEO Colette Kress is expected to rein in deals after a stretch in which Nvidia aggressively financed computing deals and startup investments.”

Or, as Dave Troy just put it, “Jensen Huang’s management style [was] indistinguishable from that of a young male trader1 that goes all-in with YOLO bets until he blows up. No risk mitigation, no concern over consequences, serial compounding poor judgment, surrounded by enablers, and eventual collapse.”

The opening phases of that collapse has come, and it is sending shockwaves throughout the industry.

The other big issue, which I have harped on endlessly, is that the LLM companies still lack a technical moat and hence are still struggling to be profitable. That was true in 2023 when I first wrote about it, and remains true.

From the jump, that has always meant that Nvidia would not be able to sustain its phenomenal growth indefinitely, because you can’t sell chips endlessly if the companies buying those chips aren’t making profits from those chips. And mostly they weren’t.

Furthermore, there are hints in the last few days that some funny business in Asia may already have been making things look better than they really were. With all the circular financing, it was always hard to tell what was really happening.

Either way, it pretty much looks like game over for the current AI bubble. (The outcome of the midterms no doubt contributed too; everybody knows it is also game over for the free pass that all the AI companies had in the first half of the Trump era; already ).

The critical question now is whether any of this affects Thursday’s planned IPO for Anthropic, or whether people will simply leap from one fad to another, the way they leapt from crypto to NFTs to generative AI. Certainly the financial fundamentals don’t justify the hoped-for valuation, once the liabilities are factored in, and selling a deeply speculative IPO in the midst of what looks like a crash of AI stocks won’t be easy.

Whether or not there are enough hopeful suckers who are bad at reading profit-and -loss (and liability!) statements out there, I cannot say.
