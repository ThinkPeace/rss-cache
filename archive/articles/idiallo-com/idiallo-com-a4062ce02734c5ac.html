GTA 6 is about to come out, and I've seen many people online joking that they'll take the week off to play it. I'm not a big fan of GTA, but the series did leave an impression on me. The last one I played was Vice City. It was an amazing game, and I still listen to its songs. It's where I first heard "Your Love" by The Outfield. I love the game, but I don't have the luxury of playing video games the way I used to.

All this to say that I understand people taking time off. When Metal Gear Solid 5 came out, I didn't just take the day off. I slowly prioritized the game over the work I was supposed to be doing. I had a contract with a client who couldn't make up his mind about a project. I delivered the project, a flower subscription service, in the span of a month. He had paid a team in Ukraine $25k to hand him a broken Drupal website. I rewrote it for half the price, and he finally had the ability to manage the website on his own.

But a website isn't complete once it's delivered. There are bugs and feature requests, or the client might just change his mind about things. So he retained me for maintenance. We launched in the summer, and he couldn't have been happier. Customers flocked in, and all was well. Now it was time for maintenance.

The problem was that the client called me every day. He wanted to change everything, all the time. My expert opinion had no effect on him, he just wanted things to be different. I was getting paid for it, so I made the changes. I was constantly on the phone with him that summer. It was annoying, but that's the life of a contractor. I could have kept going, but September 1st was the release date for MGS 5.

I could have managed if I had put my mind to it. But I had already pre-ordered the game, and I had played Ground Zeroes, the prelude that came out earlier, at least a thousand times. Nothing was going to get between me and my game.

When the game came out, I stopped answering calls. Instead, I replied to emails. Whenever the title "A Hideo Kojima Game by Hideo Kojima Produced by Hideo Himself" appeared on the screen, I used the time to respond to email. Some of the work he requested was never completed, because I knew he would change his mind. The more I ignored the client's calls, the more frustrated he became, and the more of the story I missed. "Who is Skull Face again?" I didn't want to miss a second of the dialogue.

At one point, the client called and I didn't pick up, as usual. He sent a text message: "PICK UP NOW!" I knew it was serious. So I didn't pick up right away, because doing so would have made it look like I had been intentionally ignoring him. I called him back some minutes later, only to find out it was nothing urgent. The call turned into a therapy session. He was frustrated that people had flocked to the website during the grand opening, yet it had translated into few subscriptions. The website was only a few months old, mind you.

I could have given better advice, but Quiet was shooting at me from a distance, and I hadn't figured out how to win that battle yet. Besides, I was there to do technical work, not to solve his business problems. He sensed it. When he found a bug on the website, I was eager to fix it. When he wanted to discuss ways to make the subscriptions more appealing to customers, I checked out.

Eventually, he confronted me and asked whether I was all in on this business. I wasn't. I was busy thinking that the Huey character design was lazy. Why did they recycle Otacon? Couldn't they come up with something new instead of playing on our nostalgia? Anyway, I knew a couple of friends who had just started a web consulting business. They could solve business problems and do the development work, so they would be more helpful than I was. Plus, it wouldn't make sense for him to retain me while working with them.

I found them their very first client, and the client found a better team.

My consulting pattern used to be to deliver a job and then, during the maintenance phase, look for other clients. That way, I never had downtime. But this time, I used the downtime to play my game. Remember, this was 2015. $13k went so much further then than it does now.

You can imagine my disappointment when the game ended abruptly, as if the producer had been kicked out mid-development. Kojima had been ousted from Konami. Still, it was a good game. But I needed money to survive, so for my next contract, I ended up charging $18,000 for a single static HTML page.

What I mean is, as an MGS fan, I understand the GTA fans.
