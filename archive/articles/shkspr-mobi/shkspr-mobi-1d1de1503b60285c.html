How do you make it clear what copyright licence a blog post uses? I know in our wonderful AI-powered future that copyright and copyleft are but a distant memory. But some of us still believe it's worth making these things clear to both men and machines.

The first port of call is the page's metadata. Using the rel attribute. Placing something like this in your page's <head> indicates that the copyright information can be found at a specific URl.

<link rel="license" href="https://example.com/copyright">

But that's not overly specific. Someone (or something) has to visit that website and then interpret what it says. How about we use some fancy Schema.org metadata to make things slightly clearer?

Here's a minimum viable example you can paste into the validator:

<body itemscope itemtype=https://schema.org/Blog>
    <main itemprop=blogPost itemscope itemtype=https://schema.org/BlogPosting>
        <article>
            <span itemprop="author copyrightHolder"
                  itemscope 
                  itemtype=https://schema.org/Person>
                <span itemprop=name>Terence Eden</span>
            </span>
            <a href=https://shkspr.mobi/blog/copyright-and-copyleft/ itemprop=license>
                <img alt="🄯 CC BY-SA" 
                     content="Creative Commons Attribution ShareAlike" 
                     itemprop=copyrightNotice 
                     src="https://example.com/cc-by-sa.svg">
            </a>

That says this website is a blog. The <main> element is a blog post. The article has an author who is also the copyright holder (they may be two different entities). The link is to the licence URl. The image shows the copyright notice with human readable text also available.

In other news, this blog is now CC BY-SA!
