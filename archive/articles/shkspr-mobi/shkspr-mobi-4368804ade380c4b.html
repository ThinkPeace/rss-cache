The two-part epic "Angels in America" is an ambitious play for an amateur production to pull off. The director, David Gregory, has assembled a strong cast and pushed them to their limits. The three-and-a-half hour production flew by - every moment was filled with something interesting. The sound design is inventive and makes excellent use of the space, pinging angelic choruses around the Bridewell Theatre. The lighting is an integral part of the show, picking out intimate moments and illustrating just how much colour changes the tone of a scene. Even the set, which could have been barren and pedestrian, is an exercise in how to use space, height, and texture to create a rich on-stage world.

I was the only one of our group who hadn't seen the HBO mini-series or previous productions, so I had some trepidation about diving straight into the second half of the story. I needn't have worried, though the plot is rather convoluted, it is easy to get carried along by the spectacle. It is one of the most intense pieces of theatre I've seen all year, capped off by some astounding performances.

Despite the 3h 30m runtime (thankfully with two separate intervals) the show never drags. In part, the script keeps up a healthy supply of jokes to undercut the tension. It feels like the author, Tony Kushner, can't go five lines without a bitchy aside or withering put down. Every time it threatens to become a soapy melodrama, it finds a new direction in which to skate off.

Every so often, overly-online people get into a tizzy about whether classics like The Rocky Horror Show are "problematic" - I think their heads would explode if they saw this. The content warning is pretty detailed:

contains strong language, sexual content/partial nudity, violence, references to illness and death, drug/alcohol use, homophobic/racist language, and scenes of supernatual intensity.

Parts of it are still as shocking as they must have been back in 1993 (although it has nothing on 1996's "Shopping and Fucking"). Naturally it is of its time. but has aged surprisingly well. Some of its politics are still as depressingly relevant as they were back then. While HIV/AIDS may no longer be the death-sentence is was, there's still to much shame and ignorance surrounding it.

My only criticism is with the original text. It is very much of the Literature school of drama. Like Shakespeare, some of the soliloquies are more to be experienced than to be understood. The tone at time is ponderous with meaning which is never quite clear. Does the play really need to be this long? There are several parts which feel like a natural and satisfying conclusion - yet still it continues. But, of course, why shouldn't the AIDS crisis get a multi-hour epic? Is it any less worthy of that than Ben-Hur?

A final special mention to costume designer Shannon M. Maddox; they've built such a gorgeously textured palette for the actors to immerse themselves in. I truly believed there was an angel on stage.

This production of Angels in America easily beats much of the West End for pure bravado and talent. Superb!

Pre- and Post-Show

I fully believe that the Pre-Show and Post-Show are an integral part of the theatrical experience. The Bridewell is very much off West End and the prices are sensible at £12 - £20 per seat. As I never tire of saying, you're asking people to pay a full month of Netflix for a couple of hours of stage entertainment. Is it worth it? The sofa at home is comfier than most theatre seats and there's rarely a queue for the loos.

The Bridewell is very much an "arts venue" rather than a theatre. The seats are fold-out but - thank the maker - are cushioned and padded! An absolute necessity for such a long play. The toilet provision was excellent, and they were a good deal cleaner than many bigger theatres'. The bar is a hidden gem, albeit a little musty.

I'm consistently disappointment in West End souvenir programmes. This production's programme is the best value for money I've seen in a long time. For £3 you get a newspaper sized programme full of interesting articles about the play and its themes. Compare that to any West End show where you pay twice as much for an advert-filled pamphlet. I honestly can't stop raving about it!

The Bridewell is an excellent little venue and one I'm eager to re-visit.
