While on holiday in Vilnius, I realised my beard was getting too long for the impending heatwave. I popped into literally the first barber I found and asked them to shave my mane back down to summer length.

As the chair reclined leaving me supine, the barber placed a moist towelette over my eyes and proceeded to start steaming my beard while gently brushing it. As his firm hands vigourously started massaging various unguents into my luxurious facial hair, all I could think was "Am I enjoying this?"

I had little choice but to lie back and let the professional work his magic. I got the hot towel, the cold towel, the head and shoulder massage, various scented pomades, and a selection of razors carefully whisked against my whiskers.

I don't think I enjoyed it. Obviously it is nice to smell nice, and it was helpful to have someone else use professional tools to make me look neat & tidy. I don't mind when I go into a cheap coiffeuse and am shaved in five minutes - but this was close to an hour of being pampered.

I didn't have a visceral reaction or a complete meltdown. At best I'd say I tolerated it. I felt much the same when I went to get my hair dyed. Is it that I don't like strangers touching me? Do I think I'm not worthy of a little pampering and indulgence?

By contrast, I went for dental surgery a couple of years ago and found it to be a weirdly pleasant sensation. Again, I was laid flat out, my eyes were closed, and I had a small team of professionals prodding and poking at my body. For an instant, I felt both completely vulnerable and incredibly safe. I understood why some people fake medical issues and place themselves in the hands of surgeons.

To have a dedicated professional fix you feels calming and therapeutic.

Perhaps I regard my various hairs as æsthetic fripperies? To give in to vanity is unworthy and unbecoming - but to succumb to failings of the flesh is merely mortal.

I have no problems with opticians and proctologists performing their intimate procedures on me. I can't stand getting massaged, but found physiotherapy rather pleasant. The thought of getting a pedicure fills me with horror.

Is there any difference between a physio poking at my foot in order to fix it and a pedicurist sculpting my nails and filing my calluses?

My brain is obviously weird and I need to buy myself a travel-suitable beard trimmer.
