Cor! This is a great big doorstop of a space opera. It perfectly co-opts the language of pulp sci-fi without becoming pulpy. If you've read plenty of sci-fi, you'll be familiar with some of the tropes and how the author has reinvented them. There are shades of Murderbot's autistic meltdowns, the living ships from Of Monsters and Mainframes, and a million other stories.

The book flips between genres effortlessly. You're reading a space-diplomacy novel, then you're in a battle, then there's this-Earth-emotion-you-call-love, then galactic politics, then you realise you're in a ghost story. It is episodic with plenty of great cliff-hangers. The cultures are sufficiently otherworldly and the plot feels sufficiently plausible.

One of the supposed "rules" of sci-fi is that no-one cares about your world building. You don't need to explain that the economy of Rigel Ⅶ is based on gibzits, nor should you go on an extended diversion about how FTL travel works in this universe. Claire North happily and wisely doesn't give a shit about these conventions and dedicates several blissful chapters to the intricacies of the universe. At times it feels like a pop-science history book for an alternate reality.

One particularly impressive chapter is about the plethora of pronouns scattered throughout the book. Perhaps it is a little bit satirical? Either way, it's an excellent look at how languages and cultures evolve. Similarly, Russia's war of aggression against Ukraine and the tepid response of some nations is given short shrift here.

A fun and fearsome read.
