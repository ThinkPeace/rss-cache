Let p(z) be a polynomial with leading coefficient 1, i.e. a monic polynomial. What is the maximum length of the curve

{z : |p(z)| = 1}

for each degree n?

Erdős conjectured that the maximum length was obtained by the polynomial p(z) = zn − 1. He was right, as Teng Zhang proved recently.

In general the level set {z : |p(z)| = 1} need not be connected, in which case the length is the sum of the lengths of the components.

Details

The length of the maximum curve for given n is the length for the polynomial p(z) = zn − 1. The only curves obtaining the maximum length are translations and rotations of this curve.

Note that for n = 1 the maximum curve is a circle and for n = 2 the curve is the lemniscate of Bernoulli. The general curve is called the Erdős lemniscate of order n, generalizing Bernoulli’s lemniscate.

The maximum length for given n is

21/n B(1/n, 1/2n)

where B is the beta function.

The Erdős lemniscates look similar to the “rose” curves r = cos(nθ), but these curves are not polynomial level sets.
