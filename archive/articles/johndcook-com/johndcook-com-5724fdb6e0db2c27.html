It’s obvious that you could cover a cube with smaller cubes. You could, for example, take a cube with side 1 and cover it a cube of side ½ centered on each corner.

It’s not as obvious how you would cover a ball with smaller balls, but you could cover the unit ball with balls of radius ½√3 centered at the points (±½, ±½, ±½).

In fact, you could cover a ball with four smaller balls [1].

Now consider an arbitrary convex shape K in three dimensions with non-empty interior. Is it possible to cover K by copies of itself? Since K has non-empty interior, you could cover K by putting a smaller version of K around every point. And if K is compact, then this cover has a finite subcover. But there might be a lot of elements in this finite cover. What’s the minimum number possible in general? It takes eight cubes to cover a cube, so the minimum is at least 8. But could some odd shape take more?
