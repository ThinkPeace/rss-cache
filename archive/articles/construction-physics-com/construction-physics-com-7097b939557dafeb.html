Manufacturing

From the annals of manufacturing bottlenecks: producing more Patriot missiles requires, among other things, a specialized rayon fabric called Raycarb, processed by a single US company who sources it from a tiny number of companies (apparently just two), who are unable or unwilling to ramp up capacity as fast as the US would like. “Only one rayon product of this kind is approved for use in the most advanced Patriot missiles, and it comes from the Kansas factory of a single supplier, Park Aerospace. With Patriot stockpiles shrinking, the Pentagon official pushed Park for more rayon to help ramp up missile production. It soon became clear that the company’s hands were tied. The problem lay not in Kansas but thousands of miles across the Atlantic near the vineyards of Bordeaux in France. That’s where Park’s supplier, ArianeGroup—a maker of space rockets and ballistic missiles—produces the carbonized rayon fabric, which it then ships to Park for further processing. ArianeGroup had been clear that supply was limited.” [WSJ]

Manufacturing automation startup Hadrian is buying Kutztown Foundry and Machine, a company that makes castings for, among other things, nuclear submarines. [Manufacturing Dive]

The US Marine Minerals Administration, formerly the BOEM, held an auction for seabed mining rights on 31 million acres of seabed near American Samoa. [Maritime Executive]
