Bessent comes across as a far more interesting man than I gave him credit for:

But, in the past two years, Bessent has become one of the most consequential figures in the Trump Administration. A former White House official called him “the smartest person in the Administration, by far.” Jason Miller, a longtime Trump adviser, said, of the Treasury Secretary, “There’s no other person whose name comes up more often for V.P. in ’28.” Steve Bannon, Trump’s former chief strategist, went further: “If Scott Bessent wanted it, I think he could be the Republican nominee in 2028.”

Let’s take that with a grain of salt. A year ago Bannon was spouting off that it was a sure thing Trump was going to run for — and win — a third term in 2028. Bannon’s a fucking nut.

But it’s hard not to see Bessent as the most likable man in a despicable administration:

Even members of the Trump Administration have been subject to Bessent’s wrath. In the early days of Trump’s second term, when Musk and the Department of Government Efficiency were taking a chainsaw to the federal bureaucracy, Musk installed as acting I.R.S. commissioner a senior official who had publicly accused the Biden Administration of slow-walking an investigation into Hunter Biden’s taxes. Bessent confronted Musk about the move in the Oval Office. The two exchanged “fuck you”s before the argument spilled out into the hallway, where, according to Maggie Haberman and Jonathan Swan, in their book “Regime Change,” Bessent taunted Musk about his unrealized expectations for doge. “Elon, are the savings two trillion?” Bessent prodded. “One trillion? A hundred billion?” In response, Musk threw a shoulder into Bessent’s torso.

A few months later, during a dinner for top Administration officials at Executive Branch, an exclusive Georgetown club co-founded by Donald Trump, Jr., Bessent objected to the presence of Bill Pulte, the Federal Housing Finance Agency director, who, Bessent had heard, was bad-mouthing him to the President. “Why the fuck are you talking to the President about me? Fuck you,” Bessent reportedly told Pulte. “I’m gonna punch you in your fucking face.” (Later, during testimony before the Senate Finance Committee, Bessent partially disputed this report, saying, “I actually said I was going to kick his ass.”)
