My thanks to Semantical for sponsoring DF last week to promote Ivy, the to-do list that ends. Typical to-do lists run forever. Ivy runs for one day. The concept is delightfully simple, but very effective. Each morning you make of list of things to do today, adding new items or pulling from a list of “ideas”. At the end of the day, whatever was left undone from your list goes back into the pool of ideas. Tomorrow starts empty.

The concept and the app’s name are inspired by the “Ivy Lee Method”, a simple 100-year-old productivity system based on the idea of making a short prioritized list each day. You can add as many ideas as you want, and you can access your list of ideas at any time by just pulling down on the screen. Ivy never forgets anything, but the current list of tasks in front of you is never overwhelming. Start your day and your list of tasks for today is available as a Live Activity. Other features include focus sessions, Screen Time shields, and a daily journal.

Ivy is currently on iPhone (optimized for Duo, naturally), with a native Mac app in the works. It is truly native — SwiftUI, UIKit, and Meta — and integrates with both Reminders and Calendar. It’s just a beautiful app — one of those apps that’s worth trying just to feel it.
