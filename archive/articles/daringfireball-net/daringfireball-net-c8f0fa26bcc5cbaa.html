CNBC:

But Trump’s jubilation stands in stark contrast to the reaction from even his staunchest allies on Capitol Hill, who recoiled after the reinstatement and vowed to continue their investigations into Huang — whose relationship with a potential Chinese intelligence asset they fear may have jeopardized U.S. national security.

“The risk remains that there could be information that a Chinese spy knows about a key individual in the supply chain, and that’s not worth the risk,” Sen. Kevin Cramer, R-N.D., said Tuesday on CNBC’s “Squawk Box.” “We might even have to put a babysitter on Jensen Huang for crying out loud ... just to make sure that he doesn’t do something that’s going to risk the United States.”

What’s conspicuous isn’t merely that some congressional Republicans are denouncing Trump’s actions on behalf of Huang (whether on the specific national security issues related to Nvidia and China, or the general abandonment of the pre-Trump bedrock Republican principle of laissez-faire capitalism) — it’s that none, seemingly, are defending it. Here’s a report from The Washington Sun (formerly NOTUS) on Monday quoting a host of Republicans expressing some degree of opposition to Trump’s actions.

Josh Marshall at TPM (gift link):

Over the last 24 hours various Silicon Valley types have been thumping their chests over Jensen Huang’s return, lecturing Democrats about Huang’s irreplaceability and so forth. But the reality is that none of this has anything to do with Democrats. Trump’s move yesterday is wildly unpopular, and the tech oligarchs are probably the most unpopular powerful and non-imprisoned people in the country today. For anyone who has their eyes open, Trump is not only unpopular but rapidly losing power, even among fellow Republicans, in a way that would have been unimaginable even a few months ago when numerous Senate Republicans came out publicly yesterday attacking Trump’s effort to shield Huang in the ongoing chip-smuggling and spy investigation. They’re all clustering together as their power ebbs and isolation grows.
