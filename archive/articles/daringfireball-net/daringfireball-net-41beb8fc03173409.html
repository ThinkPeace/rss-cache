Andy Lin, Mackenzie Hawkins, and Kari Soo Lindberg, reporting for Bloomberg:*

A pair of Chinese firms installed Nvidia Corp.’s powerful Blackwell semiconductors in a Shanghai data center linked to Alice Huang, the woman at the heart of a controversy surrounding the US chipmaker.

The gear installed at the Yangtze River Delta Project included a GB300 NVL72 rack, among Nvidia’s most powerful products, according to a serial number specified in documents submitted to Chinese regulators in November. Shanghai Shuoyao offered the hardware as collateral to obtain a 96 million yuan ($14 million) loan from Shanghai Innovation Bank, according to filings from the lender that were reviewed by Bloomberg News.

A second firm, Shanghai Kezhan, controlled by a Tokyo-based parent, described a GB200 server — which also relies on Blackwell chips — at the facility. That description was provided in documents when it obtained a 90 million yuan loan in late 2025 from a finance-leasing firm wholly owned by a local Shanghai government agency. [...]

The submissions reveal for the first time that Nvidia’s Blackwell processors found their way into a Chinese data center that has ties to Alice Huang and Megaspeed. These processors were until recently Nvidia’s most powerful data center products, and they’ve always been illegal to ship to China without a US license.
