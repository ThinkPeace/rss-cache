Fred Lambert, writing for Electrek:

On August 7, 2018, Musk tweeted that he was “considering taking Tesla private at $420. Funding secured.” It wasn’t. Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund had shown interest, but no deal or price had been agreed, and the take-private plan was dead within three weeks.

The SEC sued Musk for securities fraud on September 27, 2018. The agency didn’t only ask for a fine. It asked the court to bar Musk from serving as an officer or director of any public company. That would have meant no more Elon at Tesla, which lines up with “you wouldn’t have Elon if it wasn’t for me” better than anything else on this list.

And then it went away fast. Two days later, the SEC announced a settlement: Musk stayed CEO, gave up the chairman title for three years, and he and Tesla each paid $20 million, $40 million in total. Tesla added two independent directors and agreed to have a lawyer pre-approve Musk’s material tweets, but the SEC never fully enforced it. Musk didn’t admit or deny anything.

The SEC was run at the time by Jay Clayton, a Trump appointee.

I’ll bet that’s what Trump was referring to. Like most of Trump’s corrupt acts, it happened right in the open.
