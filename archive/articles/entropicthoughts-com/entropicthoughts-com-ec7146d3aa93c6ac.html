Dynomight has looked at the health effects of vitamin D supplementation. The large-scale meta-analyses that have been performed conclude there is no significant effect, even though individual studies relatively consistently point in the direction of an effect. This means our failure to detect a significant effect may be due to low experiment power.

Dynomight suggests a mechanism for that: what if the low power of the existing randomised trials is because they tend to be run in countries that fortify common foods with vitamin D? That would mean, practically speaking, all arms of the trial get the treatment, which certainly would make the experiment weaker.

To investigate, Dynomight pulls out a table with the results only from studies where participants entered with relatively low values of vitamin D in their blood.

Trial All-cause mortality
Trivedi 0.90 (0.77 to 1.07)
WHI 0.92 (0.83 to 1.01)
Lyons 0.99 (0.93 to 1.05)
RECORD 0.93 (0.85 to 1.02)
