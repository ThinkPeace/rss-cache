Lisa Hanawalt's "I Can't Stop Thinking About Horses and Sex" (permalink)

The title of animator Lisa Hanawalt's new graphic memoir I Can't Stop Thinking About Horses and Sex is a perfect encapsulation of the book's contents: a frank, self-aware, funny, horny, horsey exploration of Hanawalt's life, relationships, gender, and digestive issues:

https://drawnandquarterly.com/books/i-cant-stop-thinking-about-horses-and-sex/

Hanawalt is probably best known as the character designer for Bojack Horseman, but is also much loved in animation circles for her all-too-short-lived show Tuca & Bertie:

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuca_%26_Bertie

She lived with the comedian/podcaster/showrunner Adam "Ruins Everything" Conover for 14 years, and the memoir revolves around the couple's breakup.

As the title promises, Hanawalt is also a lifelong horse-lover, who spent her childhood developing scars on her knees and calluses on the tops of her feet from endless all-fours sessions of pretending to be a horse. As an adult, she rides at the Los Angeles Equestrian Center, where she meets other horse people, who are beautifully depicted as the mix of posh sociopaths, obsessive animal-lovers, and lovable misfits (my daughter's middle school was around the corner from the LAEC and she grew up shoveling manure and tacking up at a teaching barn there; I can vouch for Hanawalt's faithful portrayal of the vibe).

Horses are Hanawalt's touchstone, the thing that brings her thoughts and emotions back into equilibrium, no matter what life throws at her. Eventually – inevitably – she acquires a horse, and just as inevitably, that horse proves to be her savior and her heartbreak. Horses are massive, majestic, impossibly powerful animals that are nevertheless forever on the verge of death, one weird disease or inexplicable fall from being maimed for life or keeling over.

The story of Hanawalt's pony Juni is intertwined with the story of her and Conover's breakup and her post-breakup relationships. Both threads of the tale are developed through beautiful, extremely funny, deeply personal vignettes that are brought to life by Hanawalt's art, which depicts bodies (animal, human, and animalistic human avatars) as hilarious, weird, and sexy all at once.

These two motifs criss-cross as we journey through Hanawalt's dating life, her childhood sexual trauma, her IBS, and her fears and hopes about her fertility (two of the major subplots involve Hanawalt's experiences with egg-freezing and bowel problems, and the way that the medical system treats women).

Hanawalt has a gift for compact, revealing character studies that leave the reader feeling both warm affection and exasperation with everyone in her life (including Hanawalt herself). It's a world without heroes and villains, where flaws and virtues are almost always flip-sides of the same coin. Everyone is crazy sometimes, including Hanawalt, and Hanawalt is self-aware enough to know when she's getting in her own way, and even why that happens, and also (just like all of us) can't always stop herself from tripping herself up.

Hanawalt's written and drawn a wise, funny, horny book that wrings comedy and insight out of trauma and melancholy. It's a gorgeous read that lingers.

Hanawalt isn't the only person who made smart, funny media out of her and Conover's breakup. Conover's comedy special about the breakup and its aftermath is called "Big Divorce Energy" and it was released the same week that Can't Stop Thinking hit the shelves:

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CVB-SUX7zgk

I saw Conover perform this set live and it was terrific – and it got even better by the time he recorded it for the special. It makes for a perfect bookend for Hanawalt's memoir, a fact that both Conover and Hanawalt affirm in a lovely, warm conversation they recorded for Conover's Factually podcast. It's an interview that raises the bar for the kind of affection and even love a mature couple can salvage from a breakup:

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BUXEDUiICM4

The book itself is from Montreal's Drawn and Quarterly, who have a well-deserved reputation for publishing quality graphic novels, especially memoirs. D&Q always puts together a beautiful physical artifact, and Can't Stop Thinking is no exception: a handsome hardcover with luxurious, thick semi-matte pages and a bookmark ribbon sewn into the binding. It's the perfect gift for any friend going through a breakup (or thinking about buying a horse).
